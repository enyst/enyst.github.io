Read the scoring rules
Match how people parse · all successful outputs

Pass: A direct, understandable task action and subject; omit redundant human or agent subjects. Do not force a verb form that changes requested status.

Fail: Generic conversational filler, unclear actor/action/subject, or misleading tense.

Put distinguishing words first · all successful outputs; compare auth, upgrade, and frontloading families

Pass: The first two meaningful words after the emoji expose the task's distinguishing topic. Necessary task verbs can count; generic wording cannot hide the distinguishing topic.

Partial: The topic is clear but appears later than the first two meaningful words.

Fail: The visible beginning gives no useful distinction from neighboring tasks.

Keep structure and vocabulary consistent · auth and upgrade families, separately within each model, arm, and repeat

Pass: Sibling titles use parallel grammatical structure and stable vocabulary for their shared action, while retaining their different objects.

Partial: Minor unexplained wording or tense changes.

Fail: Unexplained structural or lexical changes make equivalent tasks look different.

Make variation meaningful · auth, upgrade, and feedback families, separately within each model, arm, and repeat

Pass: Equivalent tasks retain a shared pattern and meaningful differences remain visible; no incidental filler or arbitrary detail makes an item seem more important.

Partial: Some arbitrary detail or inconsistent specificity without losing the key distinction.

Fail: Meaningful differences disappear or unsupported detail creates false distinctions. Equal title length is not required.

Match connotation to purpose · feedback-complaint, feedback-praise, feedback-mixed, and neutral-compare

Pass: Complaints remain negative, praise remains positive, mixed feedback retains both positive and negative aspects, and the neutral comparison acquires no invented preference.

Partial: Broad but compatible sentiment that loses a useful distinction; a neutral description of mixed feedback that omits its two sides.

Fail: A title reverses, invents, or falsely intensifies sentiment or preference.

The initial parse rule did not define partial explicitly. Its post-scoring clarification appears in the adjudication record above; the original rubric remains unchanged.

# Readable Text

Apply these principles to text that readers scan as a collection. Follow the product's length and output-format constraints. Preserve facts, uncertainty, and distinctions that affect meaning.

1. **Make the action easy to parse.** Prefer actor, action, then object. Omit an actor shared by every item and start with the action. Match tense to the reader's context: ongoing work can use "Investigating login failures"; a completed result can use "Fixed login failures". Never imply completion without evidence.
2. **Front-load distinguishing words.** Put the words that identify this item near the start, ideally within the first two words. Remove repeated openings such as "This conversation is about". Keep the action clear while bringing its specific subject forward.
3. **Reuse structure and vocabulary.** Choose one sentence pattern for comparable items and one term per concept. Avoid switching between "issue", "page", and "ticket" for the same thing. When generating one item at a time, follow a fixed template rather than inventing variety.
4. **Make variation meaningful.** Use comparable detail and length for comparable items. Change structure or add a qualifier only when the underlying difference matters. Do not make one item appear more important through arbitrary verbosity. Keep necessary qualifiers even when they break the pattern.
5. **Match verbs to purpose and evidence.** In feedback summaries, use "Users praise" for praise and "Users complain" for complaints, rather than hiding both behind "Users discuss". Retain mixed sentiment when present; never invent emotion or certainty. Keep neutral material neutral.

Before returning, scan only the beginnings: can readers tell the items apart? Then read the collection for consistent terms, parallel structure, and unsupported implications. Return the requested text, without explaining these rules.
